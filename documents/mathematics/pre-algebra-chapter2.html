Chapter 2: Introduction to Algebraic Thinking

“Algebra is the language through which we describe patterns.”

— Professor’s Guiding Principle

Up to now, mathematics has been about known quantities: 5 apples, 12 miles, –3°C. But life is full of unknowns: “How many hours must I work to earn $200?” “What speed gets me there in time?” Algebra gives us tools to name the unknown, describe relationships, and solve for what’s missing.

This chapter introduces the building blocks: variables, expressions, and the properties that let us manipulate them wisely.

2.1 Variables and Constants

  • Constant: A fixed number.
    Examples: \( 7,\ -3.2,\ \frac{1}{2},\ \pi \)
  • Variable: A symbol (usually a letter) that represents one or more numbers.
    Examples: \( x,\ y,\ t,\ n \)

Why use variables?
Instead of solving “5 + ___ = 12” over and over, we write:
\[ 5 + x = 12 \]
Now we have a general tool—not just one answer, but a method.

Real-world context:

  • \( t \) = time in hours
  • \( C \) = cost in dollars
  • \( n \) = number of students

Note:
A variable isn’t “unknown” because it’s mysterious—it’s a placeholder for values that may change or be determined later.

2.2 Algebraic Expressions and Terms

An algebraic expression combines numbers, variables, and operations—without an equals sign.

Examples:

  • \( 3x + 7 \)
  • \( 2a - 5b + 1 \)
  • \( \frac{y}{4} - 9 \)

Parts of an Expression

  • Term: A single number, variable, or product/quotient of numbers and variables.
    In \( 4x - 7 + 2y \), the terms are: \( 4x,\ -7,\ 2y \)
  • Coefficient: The numerical part of a term that includes a variable.
    In \( 4x \), the coefficient is 4.
    In \( -x \), the coefficient is –1 (since \( -x = -1 \cdot x \)).
  • Constant term: A term with no variable (just a number).
    In \( 3x + 8 \), the constant term is 8.

Key distinction:
– Expression: \( 2x + 5 \) → no solution, just a recipe
– Equation: \( 2x + 5 = 13 \) → can be solved

2.3 Evaluating Expressions

To evaluate an expression means to substitute a value for each variable and simplify using order of operations.

Example 1: Evaluate \( 4x - 3 \) when \( x = 5 \)

\[ 4(5) - 3 = 20 - 3 = 17 \]

Example 2: Evaluate \( 2a + 3b \) when \( a = -2,\ b = 4 \)

\[ 2(-2) + 3(4) = -4 + 12 = 8 \]

Example 3 (with fractions): Evaluate \( \frac{m}{2} + n \) when \( m = -6,\ n = \frac{1}{2} \)

\[ \frac{-6}{2} + \frac{1}{2} = -3 + 0.5 = -2.5 \quad \text{or} \quad -\frac{5}{2} \]

Practical use:
A phone plan costs \( C = 20 + 0.10t \), where \( t \) = minutes over 500.
If you use 620 minutes, \( t = 120 \):
\( C = 20 + 0.10(120) = 20 + 12 = \$32 \)

2.4 Simplifying Expressions Using Properties

We simplify expressions to make them easier to evaluate, compare, or solve. We use the same properties from arithmetic—but now with variables.

1. Commutative Property

  • Addition: \( a + b = b + a \) → \( x + 5 = 5 + x \)
  • Multiplication: \( ab = ba \) → \( 3x = x \cdot 3 \)

2. Associative Property

  • \( (a + b) + c = a + (b + c) \) → \( (x + 2) + 3 = x + (2 + 3) = x + 5 \)
  • \( (ab)c = a(bc) \)

3. Distributive Property → Most Important for Algebra!

\[ a(b + c) = ab + ac \]

Example:

\[ 3(x + 4) = 3x + 12 \]

With negatives:

\[ -2(5 - y) = -2 \cdot 5 + (-2) \cdot (-y) = -10 + 2y \]

Watch signs carefully!
\( - (x + 7) = -x - 7 \) (because it’s \( -1 \cdot (x + 7) \))

Combining Like Terms

Like terms have the same variable(s) raised to the same power.

  • Like: \( 3x \) and \( -5x \) → combine to \( -2x \)
  • Not like: \( 3x \) and \( 3x^2 \) → cannot combine

Example: Simplify \( 4x + 7 - 2x + 2 \)

  1. Group like terms: \( (4x - 2x) + (7 + 2) \)
  2. Simplify: \( 2x + 9 \)

Another example:

\[ 5a - 2b + 3a + b = (5a + 3a) + (-2b + b) = 8a - b \]

Why simplify?
Simplified expressions are easier to evaluate, graph, and solve.
They reveal the essential structure of a relationship.

Chapter Summary

  • Variables stand for unknown or changing quantities; constants are fixed.
  • An algebraic expression is a combination of terms involving variables and operations.
  • To evaluate, substitute values and follow order of operations.
  • Use commutative, associative, and especially distributive properties to simplify by combining like terms.

Practice Problems

  2. Evaluate \( 5a - 2b \) when \( a = 3,\ b = -1 \)
  3. Simplify: \( 6x + 9 - 3x + 2 \)
  4. Use the distributive property: \( -4(2x - 5) \)
  5. Simplify: \( 3m - (m + 4) \)
  6. Are \( 2x^2 \) and \( 2x \) like terms? Explain.
  7. The cost of printing \( n \) posters is \( C = 25 + 1.20n \). What is the cost for 50 posters?
