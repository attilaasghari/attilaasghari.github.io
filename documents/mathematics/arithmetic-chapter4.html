Chapter 4: Decimals and Percents

“Decimals are fractions in disguise. Percents are decimals wearing a hat labeled ‘per hundred.’”

— Professor’s Notebook

From checking your bank balance to comparing phone battery levels, from calculating a 20% tip to understanding inflation rates—you’re constantly working with decimals and percents. This chapter equips you with the tools to move fluently between fractions, decimals, and percents—and to solve real-life problems with confidence.

4.1 Decimal Representation and Place Value

A decimal is a way to write fractions whose denominators are powers of 10 (10, 100, 1000, etc.).

The decimal point separates the whole number part from the fractional part.

Example:

\[ 23.407 \]
Tens Ones . Tenths Hundredths Thousandths
2 3 . 4 0 7

So:

\[ 23.407 = 20 + 3 + \frac{4}{10} + \frac{0}{100} + \frac{7}{1000} \]

Key Insight:
Each place to the right of the decimal is 10 times smaller than the one before it.

Zeros matter—but not always:

  • \(0.5 = 0.50 = 0.500\) → trailing zeros after the decimal don’t change value.
  • But \(0.05 \ne 0.5\) → zeros between the decimal and digits do change value!

4.2 Converting Between Fractions, Decimals, and Percents

These three forms are equivalent representations of the same number. Mastering conversion is essential for comparison and computation.

Fraction → Decimal

Divide numerator by denominator.

\[ \frac{3}{4} = 3 \div 4 = 0.75 \] \[ \frac{1}{3} = 0.\overline{3} \quad \text{(repeating decimal)} \]

Tip: Use long division or a calculator—but understand what the digits mean.

Decimal → Fraction

Write the decimal as a fraction over its place value, then simplify.

\[ 0.6 = \frac{6}{10} = \frac{3}{5} \] \[ 0.125 = \frac{125}{1000} = \frac{1}{8} \]

Decimal → Percent

Multiply by 100 and add the % symbol.

\[ 0.45 = 45\% \quad ; \quad 1.2 = 120\% \quad ; \quad 0.007 = 0.7\% \]

Percent → Decimal

Divide by 100 (or move decimal point two places left).

\[ 82\% = 0.82 \quad ; \quad 5\% = 0.05 \quad ; \quad 150\% = 1.5 \]

Fraction ↔ Percent

Go through decimal as an intermediate step, or scale denominator to 100 if possible.

\[ \frac{7}{20} = \frac{35}{100} = 35\% \] \[ \frac{2}{3} \approx 0.666... \approx 66.7\% \]

Practical Insight:
In polls, finance, and science, percents make comparisons intuitive:
“40% of students” is clearer than “2/5 of students” in many contexts.

4.3 Operations with Decimals

The rules mirror whole-number arithmetic—but alignment matters.

Addition & Subtraction

→ Line up the decimal points.

Example:

\[ 12.3 + 4.567 = ? \]

Write as:

  12.300
+  4.567
--------
  16.867

Why? You’re adding tenths to tenths, hundredths to hundredths—just like aligning place values in whole numbers.

Multiplication

→ Ignore decimals at first, multiply as whole numbers, then count total decimal places.

Example:

\[ 0.6 \times 0.04 = ? \]
  • \(6 \times 4 = 24\)
  • Total decimal places: 1 (from 0.6) + 2 (from 0.04) = 3
  • So: \(0.6 \times 0.04 = 0.024\)

Real-world use:
Cost of 2.5 pounds of apples at $1.20 per pound:
\(2.5 \times 1.20 = 3.00\) → $3.00

Division

→ Move the decimal point in both divisor and dividend to make the divisor a whole number.

Example:

\[ 4.8 \div 0.6 = ? \]
  • Multiply numerator and denominator by 10:
    \(48 \div 6 = 8\)

Check: \(0.6 \times 8 = 4.8\) → correct.

4.4 Solving Percent Problems

Most percent problems fit one of three forms:

“What is X% of Y?”
“X is what percent of Y?”
“X is Y% of what number?”

Use the percent equation:

\[ \text{Part} = \text{Percent} \times \text{Whole} \]

(where Percent is in decimal form)

1. Finding a Percent of a Number

> “What is 18% of 250?”

\[ 0.18 \times 250 = 45 \]

✅ Answer: 45

2. Percent Increase or Decrease

> “A $80 shirt is on sale for 25% off. What’s the sale price?”

  • Discount: \(0.25 \times 80 = 20\)
  • Sale price: \(80 - 20 = 60\)

Or faster: Pay 75% of original → \(0.75 \times 80 = 60\)

3. Finding the Original Amount

> “After a 10% raise, your salary is $55,000. What was it before?”

Let original = \(x\)

\[ x + 0.10x = 1.10x = 55{,}000 \] \[ x = \frac{55{,}000}{1.10} = 50{,}000 \]

✅ Original salary: $50,000

4. Tax, Tip, and Markup

Same as percent increase:

  • Tax: \( \text{Total} = \text{Price} \times (1 + \text{tax rate}) \)
  • Tip: \( \text{Total} = \text{Bill} \times (1 + \text{tip rate}) \)

Life skill:
Always ask: Is this percent applied to the original or the new amount?
(e.g., “20% off, then 10% off” ≠ “30% off”!)

Chapter Summary

  • Decimals extend place value to the right of the decimal point.
  • Fractions ↔ Decimals ↔ Percents: Convert using division, place value, or scaling to 100.
  • Decimal operations:
    • Align points (add/sub)
    • Count places (multiply)
    • Shift decimals (divide)
  • Percent problems: Use the equation Part = Percent × Whole, and watch for increase/decrease contexts.
